02 — The basics

What is computer science?

Solving problems with computers. In practice, most of the work follows this cycle.

  1. 01

    Understand

    What's the problem, and who has it?

  2. 02

    Design

    Plan the approach before writing code.

  3. 03

    Build

    Write the code.

  4. 04

    Test

    Find what's broken and fix it (debugging).

  5. 05

    Ship

    Release it to real users.

  6. 06

    Learn

    Measure what happened, then improve it.

Repeat
This is the same cycle at a class project, a startup, or Netflix. The loop never really ends.

04 — The major

What you study

  • ProgrammingWriting code in languages like Python, Java, and C++.
  • Data structures & algorithmsHow to organize information and solve problems efficiently. This is what most interviews test.
  • MathDiscrete math, calculus, statistics, and linear algebra.
  • SystemsHow computers, operating systems, and networks work underneath.
  • ElectivesAI, security, graphics, databases. This is where you pick a direction.
My favorite class

Data Structures. It's where coding started to feel like real problem solving.

My hardest class

Computer Architecture. I just pushed through it. Not every class clicks, and that's okay.

A degree is usually 4 years. You don't need to know how to code before you start.

08 — Honestly

What people don't tell you about CS

  • LeetCode

    Most software interviews include timed coding problems. It's a separate skill from your classes, and most people practice it for months.

    My experience I practiced steadily for months before my interviews.

  • Vibe coding

    AI tools can write a lot of code now. You still have to understand it to review it, fix it, and notice when it's wrong.

    My experience I use it a lot, and the industry uses it even more. But the code is only as good as the engineer: it can make a good engineer exceptional, and make a weak one look even worse.

  • Recruiting starts early

    Summer internships often open the fall before, sometimes a full year ahead.

    My experience For me it came through the Netflix × Formation fellowship and networking.

  • Rejection is normal

    Most applications get no response. Everyone you admire has a pile of rejections.

    My experience Lots of no's. I kept trying.

  • Classes aren't enough

    Projects, clubs, hackathons, and programs often matter as much as grades.

    My experience What helped me most: INIT and other clubs, projects, and the community around me.

  • Feeling behind

    Almost everyone feels like they know less than the people around them.

    My experience I started with no coding experience while others had years. I caught up by building projects.

This is the most personal part. Spend time here.

09 — Getting started

What you can do now

At school

Take a class

AP Computer Science Principles or AP CS A if your school offers them. Join or start a coding or robotics club.

On your own

Learn a language

Python is a good first one. Free options: Harvard's CS50, freeCodeCamp, Khan Academy.

With other people

Build things

Hackathons, summer programs like Girls Who Code, and small projects you actually care about.

Ask for help

Find a mentor

Someone a few steps ahead of you: a teacher, an older student, someone from a club. Having one really helps.

What I wish I'd done in high school
Tried coding earlier. Even something small, just to know if I liked it.

Point out that most of this is free.
